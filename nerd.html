What is NERD?

NERD (Net Estimated Rating, Denoised) is our all-in-one player impact metric: an estimate of how many points per 100 possessions a player adds to his team, split into offense (O-NERD) and defense (D-NERD). It combines two kinds of evidence, each weighted by its reliability: what actually happens on the scoreboard when the player is on the floor (a multi-year, luck-adjusted RAPM in which every player in the league is estimated jointly), and what his statistical profile suggests (a box-score prior fit against long-run impact). Thin samples lean on the profile; large samples earn their own number.

The Denoised part is what makes it ours: every possession behind NERD runs through this site's luck-adjustment pipeline — opponent three-point variance and other scoreboard noise are muted before the model ever sees the data, and all stint scores are calibrated to official box scores. Playoff possessions are included in the on-court evidence; the statistical profile uses regular-season stats only.

The ± column is an honest uncertainty band (one standard deviation) from a Kalman filter that tracks each player through his career with aging curves fit on 14,600 player-seasons. Small samples have wide bands — that is the point.

How good is it? (validation)

On the retrodiction benchmark published by dunksandthrees (prior-season ratings, weighted by next-season minutes, predicting next-season team net rating), NERD's error (2.33) beats BPM (2.60) and RAPTOR (2.75) measured on the identical pipeline — a larger margin over those anchors than EPM reports over them on its own test. In a 12-season game-margin backtest (~14,000 games), NERD is the most accurate public-style metric tested (MAE 9.50 vs RAPTOR 9.63, BPM 9.68; Vegas closing lines 9.20). Season-level values here are fixed at season end; no in-season information leaks in.
